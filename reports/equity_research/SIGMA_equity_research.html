Verdict

Quality: genuinely strong unit economics, with real and currently unresolved credibility gaps. Value: balanced, not a clear mispricing either way.

Sigma Solve clears the coffee-can gates cleanly — a 9/9 "9iner" — and cumulative cash generation exceeds cumulative profit once this pipeline's own capex-proxy distortion is corrected (harder cash test: +₹86.4 Cr over the decade). Nearly all of its revenue is booked through a wholly-owned US subsidiary that was, until FY2023-24, roughly 40% owned by the promoter's own relatives — a structure that reached full ownership through a subsidiary-level buyback costing the parent nothing in cash, and appears, on the facts available, to have favoured public shareholders.

The problem is not fraud or self-dealing. It is that EBITDA margin has fallen from 40.5% (FY2023) to 29.5% (FY2026), every specific guidance commitment management made on its last two calls has since been missed, and the company has not held an investor call since February 2024 — independently confirmed via Screener's own concall archive. Two SEBI adjudication orders landed in 2025: a modest, company-specific IPO-proceeds compliance lapse, and a separate, apparently still-open investigation into suspected fraudulent trading in the stock by outside entities unconnected to the company.

Run through a reverse DCF, ₹44.20 requires either a partial margin recovery (to ~34%, roughly halfway back to the FY2023 peak) or continued growth at the company's own recent, realised pace — neither a stretch on the historical record, neither confirmed as currently happening. A genuinely good business, real and current credibility gaps, and a price that sits in a real grey zone rather than an obvious mispricing.

01The business, and an unusual way of booking revenue

A tiny, founder-led Ahmedabad software exporter whose income is billed almost entirely through its own US subsidiary — a structure worth understanding before anything else

Sigma Solve Limited was incorporated in Ahmedabad in 2010 by promoter Prakash Parikh, listed on NSE's SME platform in October 2020, and migrated to the NSE/BSE mainboard in June 2023 — a genuinely new mainboard name, with a listed history of barely three years on the market this report values it in. The company describes itself as an enterprise software consultancy: custom application development, web and e-commerce development, business intelligence and analytics, CRM development, digital marketing, UI/UX design, and automation testing/QA, increasingly organised around a small number of industry verticals (logistics, construction technology, process automation, and a newly-started Microsoft Dynamics practice) rather than as a generalist body-shop. Promoters Prakash and Kalpana Parikh together control 73.2% of the company; institutional ownership is effectively zero (FII and DII combined under 0.1%).

The structure that matters more than any other fact in this report

Sigma Solve's own FY2025 annual report states plainly: "Our Company do not have any identified customers being almost 100% of sales are booked by Sigma Solve Inc., and subsequently, our company 'Sigma' is charging on monthly basis to Sigma Solve Inc. for providing the execution facilities on per man hour basis." In other words, the Ahmedabad-listed entity does not sell to end clients directly — it bills its own wholly-owned US subsidiary, Sigma Solve Inc. (Florida), for developer hours, and Sigma Solve Inc. is the entity that actually holds the client relationships, signs the contracts, and (per management) drives sales, marketing and delivery leadership from the US.

That subsidiary was not always wholly owned. Sigma Solve Ltd acquired a 51.04% stake in Sigma Solve Inc. in 2019; by the FY2023 annual report the parent's holding was 59.81% — meaning roughly 40% of the entity that generates essentially all of the group's revenue was, for years, held outside the listed company, by its own co-founders Biren Zaverchand and Prerak Parikh, both relatives of the promoter family. That stake reached 100% only during FY2023-24, through a mechanism examined in full in Section 11. Prerak Parikh was appointed a formal Director of Sigma Solve Ltd only on 30 June 2025 — meaning he had been presented to investors as "management" on earnings calls for at least a year and a half before holding a board seat.

Two further entities round out the current group structure: Rish Info Logistics Private Limited (70%-owned, incorporated April 2024 — the vehicle for the logistics-vertical SaaS ambitions management has discussed on calls) and Sigma Accounting Private Limited (a 33% associate incorporated August 2023 for accounting-software development, which the company exited in December 2025). The company also approved a 10-for-1 stock split (face value ₹10 to ₹1) during FY2025, which is reflected in all per-share figures in this report.

An extraordinary return on a genuinely thin capital base

Because the business needs almost no fixed assets — a services company's real asset is people, and the group owns only a small Ahmedabad office property beyond that — capital employed has stayed small even as profit has scaled. ROCE has run 40-75% over the last five years on this pipeline's own calculation, among the highest this pipeline has measured, though the trend has been declining as the balance sheet's treasury book (retained profit invested in mutual funds and deposits, given a dividend payout frozen for four straight years — Section 10) has grown faster than operating profit. Section 02 works through what that combination does and does not tell you about durability, and Section 04 covers the real margin compression that has accompanied the revenue growth since FY2023.

02A services-business checklist, applied

Sigma Solve passes the headline gates cleanly. The structural and disclosure questions underneath them are where this report spends most of its time.

A tiny, founder-led services exporter fails differently from a capital-intensive industrial or a consumer marketplace. There is no order book worth overstating (Sigma Solve's own disclosed pipeline runs to a few hundred thousand dollars — smaller than one month of revenue), no float, and no treasury book large enough to distort return ratios the way it does at bigger companies on this pipeline. The risks here are narrower and sharper: whether the near-total dependence on a single revenue-booking subsidiary is fully disclosed, whether reported profit turns into collected cash as receivable days lengthen, and whether management's own guidance is a reliable guide to what actually gets delivered.

The two lines in that table that matter more than the others

A 9iner coffee-can tier with a cumulative cash-conversion ratio above 1× looks, at a glance, like an unusually clean small-cap. It is — on the numbers the module computes by default. What that first pass does not surface is the same distortion this pipeline has now found on more than one treasury-heavy company: a capex proxy contaminated by investment purchases, which here would have manufactured a near-failing cash test out of a business that, once corrected, comfortably self-funds. That correction matters, but it is a data-quality fix, not a finding about the business — the more important line in the table is the last one but two: guidance credibility. Every other row in this scorecard describes a company that looks, mechanically, like a clean small-cap compounder. The guidance record describes a management team whose specific, quantified, multi-year commitments — both on revenue and on margin — have not held up under the one real test available: what a later quarter's actuals showed against an earlier call's promise. That gap, not the accounting technicalities, is this report's central finding.

Two things in that table worth stopping on

First, FY2023 was the peak year on almost every measure — OPM 40.5%, PBT margin 42.4%, and the tightest receivable cycle (35 days) since the company's earliest, tiniest years. That combination coincided with the year Sigma Solve Inc. became a wholly-owned subsidiary (Section 11) — a fact worth holding in mind rather than treating as a coincidence, since a chunk of the FY2024 PAT growth the company highlighted on its Q3 FY24 call ("PAT attributable to owners grew by 59% year-over-year... as a result of our successful group simplification") reflects removing minority interest from the P&L, not operating improvement.

Second, the FY2023→FY2026 slide is real and multi-dimensional, not a single bad quarter. OPM fell 40.5%→29.5% (a decline of more than a quarter of its peak level) over three straight years, while receivable days more than doubled from 35 to a peak of 100 before easing to 70. Both moved in the same direction at the same time as revenue kept growing at a respectable 16-25% a year — exactly the "record revenue, compressing margin, lengthening collection cycle" pattern that this pipeline's other reports have found is common, not rare, among small Indian IT-services exporters navigating FY2025-26 (Section 07). The margin bridge below decomposes the FY2023-to-FY2026 move precisely.

Cumulative operating cash flow over the period this pipeline can measure (₹98.0 Cr) exceeds cumulative profit (₹85.1 Cr) — a 1.15× ratio that clears the standard cash-conversion gate. But that headline number smooths over real year-to-year unevenness: only 5 of 10 individual years cleared the gate on their own, FY2020's cash flow was outright negative against a positive profit, and the two most recent margin-compression years (FY2024 at 0.46×, FY2025 at 0.94×) both fell short — directly traceable to the receivable-days spike documented in Section 05.

04Margins: a real decline, and no management explanation on record for it

The last time this company spoke to investors, it promised margins would hold or improve. They have fallen every year since — and there has been no call since to ask why.

EBITDA margin fell from a peak of 40.5% (FY2023) to 29.5% (FY2026) — a decline of more than a quarter of its peak level, spread across three consecutive years, not a single bad quarter. Unlike every other company on this pipeline, this report cannot show what management says caused it, because the company has not held an investor call since 9 February 2024 — the entire margin decline documented in Section 03 happened after the last time Sigma Solve spoke to the investors who own its stock. This is independently confirmed via Screener.in's own concall archive, not merely an artefact of this pipeline's document collection.

What the record shows, and what it contradicts

On the two calls that exist, both held while margin was still near its peak, management was directly asked whether the then-prevailing 35-40% margin was sustainable, and answered with specific confidence:

"I strongly feel they are sustainable because for us to even get to these kinds of top lines and continue to grow, we also have to get into higher end technologies. And when you get into those, the cost goes up, but at the same time, what you can demand a premium for those services as well. So yes, the cost will go up but I feel that the value of the services also goes higher."

Prerak Parikh, Co-founder, Sigma Solve Inc. — Management Interaction call, 1 December 2023

"Obviously they are not going to come with lower salaries, but those expectations and that constant crunch and the attrition that has very much levelled out... our margins are going to be a little better."

Prerak Parikh — same call, on wage-inflation pressure specifically

Both statements have been contradicted by what actually happened: margin did not hold, and it did not improve — it fell in every one of the three fiscal years since. That is not, on its own, proof of bad faith; industry-wide pricing and cost pressure on small Indian IT-services exporters is real and documented at other companies this pipeline has covered (Section 07). What is unusual, and worth stating plainly, is that no later call exists to update, walk back, or explain the miss — the record simply goes silent at the exact point the company's own confident guidance started to diverge from its results.

What this report can and cannot rule out

Per this pipeline's binding rule on anomalies: an unexplained move gets a sourced cause, or an explicit statement that no cause was found. Here, none was found in any retrieved filing, transcript, or news item covering the FY2024-FY2026 period specifically. Plausible candidates exist — the same industry-wide AI-driven pricing pressure on effort-based billing this pipeline has documented at other small Indian IT-services exporters (Section 07), continued investment in the new logistics and Microsoft Dynamics verticals mentioned on the last call, or simply normal competitive margin normalisation from an unusually high FY2023 peak — but none of these is confirmed by a primary source, and this report does not present any of them as the explanation. The honest position is that the cause is unknown because the company has not been asked, and has not said.

Through FY2022-23, the company was in an unusually favourable position — negative net working capital, meaning customers and the payment cycle were effectively funding operations rather than the other way round, with receivable days as low as 29. That reversed sharply and quickly: net working capital swung from −₹15.9 Cr (FY2023) to +₹25.5 Cr (FY2026) in three years, and the incremental working-capital ratio for FY2024 alone — 314% of that year's incremental revenue — means working capital grew more than three times faster than sales did that year, an extreme, one-year spike rather than a gradual drift.

This tracks precisely with the margin decline documented in Section 04, and it suffers from the identical evidentiary problem: no management explanation exists on record, because the FY2024 spike happened in the same window the company stopped holding investor calls. A slower-paying client mix, a specific large receivable dispute, or simply the mechanical effect of the group-simplification accounting change around the Sigma Solve Inc. consolidation (Section 11) are all plausible candidates; none is confirmed. The FY2026 easing (100 days → 70 days) is a genuinely positive signal that whatever drove the FY2024-25 spike may be resolving, but a single year of improvement after a two-year deterioration is not yet a trend, and there is no primary source explaining that improvement either.

What is not in dispute

Whatever the cause, cumulative cash generation over the full period remains ahead of cumulative profit (Section 02) — this is a real, sizeable swing in the working-capital cycle, not evidence that reported profit is fictitious. It is the kind of unexplained multi-year move that a company holding regular investor calls would ordinarily be asked about directly; this one has not been, because there has been no call to ask it on.

06How Sigma Solve compares with its real peers

No listed Indian company matches its scale, its structure and its business mix all at once — the peer set here is a spectrum, not a tight match

Sigma Solve names no competitor anywhere in its filings. Twenty-one candidates were researched to build the set below; roughly half were dropped outright for a genuinely different business model (defence-electronics manufacturing, e-waste recycling, ad-tech, BFSI staffing, pharmaceuticals) or outright financial distress unrelated to the operating comparison this report needs. What is left is a real constraint worth stating plainly: almost every listed Indian IT-services company is larger than Sigma Solve, and the one company found that most precisely replicates its unusual "Indian company billing 100% through a wholly-owned US subsidiary" structure — Trigyn Technologies — is roughly ten times its size and financially much weaker today.

The closest match on scale is the closest warning on margin durability

Alphalogic Techsys is the only listed company found smaller than Sigma Solve, with a near-identical promoter holding (73.8% vs. 73.2%) — the best available answer to "who else is actually this size." Its net margin runs meaningfully thinner (~15% against Sigma Solve's 25%+), and its stock has derated roughly 49% over the past year, worth understanding before leaning on it as reassurance rather than caution. Sagarsoft (India) is the more pointed cautionary comparable: the same broad model — US-facing IT services, similar revenue scale — has gone from profitable to a −₹15 Cr net loss in its most recent year, illustrating concretely what margin and pricing-power failure looks like for a company built the way Sigma Solve is.

Virtual Galaxy Infotech and Exato Technologies: the closest quality match, with one important caveat

Both companies sit at roughly twice Sigma Solve's scale with genuinely comparable margin and return quality (Virtual Galaxy's 33.7% ROCE is the closest any peer comes to Sigma Solve's own number). The caveat that matters: both serve identifiable domestic clients (BFSI/government software, customer-experience services) rather than booking revenue through a single, opaque, wholly-owned offshore subsidiary — the specific structural risk theme this report treats as central (Section 11) simply does not exist for either of them. They are useful for benchmarking growth and margin, not for benchmarking disclosure quality.

Trigyn Technologies: the one structural match, and a warning about where this model can end up

Trigyn Technologies is the only listed company found that replicates Sigma Solve's specific structure — an Indian parent billed against, and consolidated through, a wholly-owned US subsidiary (Trigyn Technologies Inc., New Jersey) that holds the actual client relationships. It is roughly ten times Sigma Solve's revenue and has been for years, but its profitability has collapsed — net profit fell from ₹39 Cr (FY2022) to ₹2 Cr (FY2026), ROCE now sits at 2.3%, essentially break-even. This is not offered as a prediction that Sigma Solve will follow the same path; the two companies' recent trajectories (accelerating margin compression here, a multi-year structural decline there) look different in shape. It is offered as the only available evidence on what this specific "single offshore billing subsidiary" structure looks like once growth stops paying for it.

Silver Touch Technologies: the closest qualitative and geographic match

Silver Touch Technologies — also Ahmedabad-based, also promoter-controlled (74.7%), also SME-platform-IPO heritage, of a similar company vintage — is the best "same city, same era, same ownership structure" comparable, at roughly three times Sigma Solve's scale. Its business mix (SAP implementation and e-governance for identifiable enterprise and government clients) is more diversified and transparent than Sigma Solve's single-subsidiary export model, which is itself the point of the comparison: two companies that started from a similar place have ended up with very different disclosure profiles.

Set against this spread, Sigma Solve's 40.4% current-year ROCE and 28.0% ROE remain at or near the top of the set — but, as Section 02 already notes, both are on a declining trend from a materially higher peak, and none of the peers with genuinely comparable numbers (Virtual Galaxy, Exato) share the specific structural feature that makes Sigma Solve's own numbers harder to verify against an identifiable customer base.

07Growth outlook: a real niche, a new logistics bet, and the same AI-pricing question this pipeline keeps finding

Demand for the underlying work is genuine. Whether Sigma Solve captures it at its historical margin is the open question — and unlike this pipeline's other reports, there is no recent management commentary to weigh against the external evidence

The demand case is not the constraint

Sigma Solve sits in the same broad category this pipeline's industry research has already mapped for a comparable small Indian IT-services exporter: NASSCOM puts India's total tech-industry revenue at ~$315bn for FY26 (+6.1% y-o-y), with the specific digital-engineering / custom-software-development niche consistently flagged by NASSCOM, Zinnov and third-party market research as growing several multiples faster (~20% range) than that headline figure. Nothing in this niche's demand backdrop explains Sigma Solve's own margin and receivable-cycle deterioration (Sections 04, 05) — the addressable market for offshore enterprise software development, US-facing, is not shrinking.

The same AI-pricing-pressure question this pipeline has now found repeatedly

The large Indian IT majors are already disclosing measurable pricing deflation from generative-AI coding tools in FY26 earnings calls — HCLTech's CEO has described a contract that would once have been priced at "$100 million" now going for "maybe $80 million" under client productivity-based pricing demands. Motilal Oswal's sell-side view: roughly 12-15% of Indian IT-sector revenue faces direct AI-driven displacement risk, with productivity gains "extracted almost entirely by enterprise buyers through productivity demands and vendor price pressure" rather than captured by the vendor as margin. Two matched-scale Indian IT-services boutiques this pipeline has already examined — InfoBeans Technologies and Kellton Tech Solutions, both sub-₹600 Cr revenue, developer-time-billed — showed the identical "record revenue, compressing margin" pattern in the same FY26 quarters. Sigma Solve's own three-year margin slide (Section 04) is consistent with this broader, category-wide pattern, though — critically — no management commentary exists to confirm or rule this out as the actual cause, because the company has not held a call since before most of this pricing pressure became visible industry-wide (Section 08).

The one concrete new growth vehicle: Rish Info Logistics

On both available calls, management pointed to a logistics-industry vertical — a SaaS platform for transportation management and route optimisation — as a specific product-led growth ambition, distinct from the core billed-hours business. That ambition now has a concrete corporate form: Rish Info Logistics Private Limited, a 70%-owned subsidiary incorporated in April 2024 (Section 11). No standalone financial disclosure for this entity was found in any source reviewed, so this report cannot say whether it is generating meaningful revenue, remains pre-revenue, or has been scaled back — an open item for a future update, and a genuine gap given management's own past framing of logistics-vertical SaaS as a margin-accretive differentiator ("if anything, it's only going to boost those margins up," Prerak Parikh, December 2023).

What would resolve this section, and why it currently cannot be resolved

The evidentiary gap this section cannot close on its own

Every other report on this pipeline that has examined a margin-compression episode could weigh management's own explanation against external evidence and score the two against each other. This report cannot, because the compression happened entirely inside a window with no primary management commentary. What would resolve it: (1) a new investor call, at any point, addressing the margin and working-capital trends directly; (2) any standalone financial disclosure for Rish Info Logistics showing whether the logistics-SaaS ambition has produced revenue; (3) a subsequent quarter's realised billing rates or per-developer revenue, the cleanest available test of whether AI-driven pricing pressure is the operative mechanism here as it appears to be at comparable-scale peers; (4) resolution of the open SEBI "Delhi Group" trading investigation (Section 11), which bears on confidence in the stock's price discovery generally, if not on the operating business directly.

08What outside scrutiny exists — essentially none

No broker covers this stock, no forum has ever discussed it seriously, and the company itself has not taken a question from an investor in two and a half years

Zero institutional coverage

Investing.com's consensus-estimates page for Sigma Solve confirms it is covered by 0 analysts. A search for brokerage initiation notes, target prices, or independent research write-ups returned nothing beyond data-aggregator pages (Screener, Tickertape, TradingView and similar) carrying live ratios with no analytical commentary. This is unsurprising for an ~$11M-revenue company three years into a mainboard listing, but it means every valuation view in Section 13 is this report's own, with no external institutional check to weigh against it.

The retail forum came close to the right question once, and never followed up

Sigma Solve has never had a dedicated thread on ValuePickr — a genuine rarity for a company covered on this pipeline. Its entire retail-forum footprint is eight posts across three threads, none company-specific. The one substantive exchange, in a general Q&A megathread in November 2023, has a retail investor flagging exactly the structural question this report treats as central: how did the parent's stake in Sigma Solve Inc. rise from 59.81% to 100% "with no outflow of cash"? The answer he received explained the buyback mechanics correctly but never asked the more pointed question — who were the other shareholders, and why was the entity carrying nearly all of the group's revenue not wholly owned by the listed company in the first place? That question was never asked by anyone, on that thread or any other. A single ChatGPT-generated restatement of the same mechanical explanation appears a month later; nothing else follows. No promoter-shareholding trend, valuation debate, order-book discussion, or competitor comparison exists anywhere on the forum for this stock.

The company has not spoken to investors since February 2024

Five investor interactions took place between December 2022 and February 2024, each with a transcript and presentation archived on Screener.in. None has taken place since — independently confirmed via Screener's own concall history, not merely an absence in this pipeline's document collection. The silence began at almost exactly the point margin and working capital started moving against the company (Sections 04, 05), and it directly contradicts a specific commitment made on the record: "You'll get an update every quarter... we'll try to do conference call at least half yearly if not every quarter," said by the company's own IR advisor at the close of the December 2023 call. That has not happened even once since. Scored explicitly in Section 09.

Put together: no analyst asks Sigma Solve hard questions, no forum applies sustained scrutiny, and the company has stopped giving anyone the opportunity to ask on a call. Whatever this report cannot verify about the company's recent performance is not unverifiable by choice — it is unverifiable because the normal channels through which a small-cap earns outside scrutiny are all, for different reasons, empty.

The pattern, stated plainly

This is not a management team whose promises were vague enough to escape testing — the opposite. That is a defensible, if unexciting, use of a small company's retained capital — but it also means shareholders have not yet been shown what management intends to do with an accumulating cash pile beyond letting it sit in short-term investments.

11Governance: two real SEBI orders in 2025, and one structural question the standard audit doesn't reach

Neither order implicates promoter self-dealing. Both are worth understanding precisely rather than either dismissed or overstated

SEBI order one: a real, regulator-confirmed compliance lapse on IPO proceeds

On 25 September 2025, SEBI issued a joint adjudication order against Sigma Solve Limited and Beeline Broking Limited (the company's SME-IPO merchant banker). The finding: Sigma Solve spent ₹151.57 lakh — 30.6% of total IPO proceeds — on "General Corporate Purpose," against a 25% cap disclosed in its own prospectus. Specifically, ₹31.57 lakh originally earmarked for public-issue expenses was instead redirected to pay down an overdraft used to fund the purchase of the company's Ahmedabad office — the same office-property purchase visible independently in this pipeline's own balance-sheet data as the FY2023 capital-work-in-progress and FY2024 net-block jump (Section 03). The order also cites a 50-day delay in filing a required proceeds-deviation statement (for the half-year ended March 2021) and inadequate Director's Report disclosure of the variation.

Two things are worth holding at once here. First, this is real: a public company breached its own IPO prospectus's spending cap and disclosed the deviation late. Second, SEBI's own order explicitly found that the later reallocation did not render the original prospectus disclosures misleading — citing a 2021 precedent on exactly this distinction — and the penalty imposed on Sigma Solve was ₹2 lakh, small in absolute terms against ₹28 Cr of FY2026 operating profit. This reads as a genuine but modest compliance failure, the kind many small SME-heritage IPOs commit in their early years, not a disclosure-fraud finding.

SEBI order two: an open investigation into trading in the stock — not into the company

A separate order, dated 28 August 2025, concerns SEBI's investigation into suspected fraudulent or manipulative trading in Sigma Solve's own listed shares, under the standard Prohibition of Fraudulent and Unfair Trade Practices framework. This order does not name Sigma Solve Limited, its promoters, or any director as a noticee. The party actually penalised — ₹1 lakh — is Capital Finance and Investments LLP, fined not for manipulation itself but for repeatedly failing to appear before SEBI's investigators despite three summonses. SEBI's order describes that firm as suspected of links to a group it calls "Delhi Group entities," citing large fund transactions connected to an individual named Kulbhushan Parashar and call records connecting the firm to a second individual, Manish Kumar.

What this is, and what this report will not claim

No source found in this research implicates Sigma Solve Limited, the Parikh family, or any company director in the trading activity under investigation, and no order resolving the underlying manipulation question — whether the scrip was actually subject to fraudulent trading, and by whom — was found. What is confirmed is narrower but still material: SEBI considered trading in this specific, thinly-held micro-cap's stock suspicious enough to open a formal investigation, tied it to a named group of outside entities, and that investigation appears to remain open rather than resolved as of the most recent order found. For a stock with under 0.1% institutional ownership and zero analyst coverage (Section 08), an active regulatory question mark over the integrity of its own trading is a fact worth knowing, independent of whether the company itself is ever implicated.

The structural question the standard RPT lens doesn't reach

The existing related-party audit rates Sigma Solve 1/3 ("Watch") — every AOC-2 not-at-arm's-length disclosure reads "NA," and the only related-party items found across FY2023-25 are ordinary intercompany billing with the (now wholly-owned) US subsidiary, KMP remuneration, and two small entities (Sigma Solve Australia Pty Ltd, Sigma Accounting Pvt Ltd) with negligible transaction values. That audit is accurate on its own terms. What it does not capture is the single largest capital event in the company's history: Sigma Solve Inc. — the entity that books essentially all of the group's revenue — was, until FY2023-24, roughly 40% owned by the promoter's own relatives (co-founders Biren Zaverchand and Prerak Parikh), and reached 100% ownership through a subsidiary-level share buyback that cost the parent company nothing in cash (Section 01). Because this was structured as a buyback at the subsidiary level rather than a direct contract between the listed company and a related party, it falls outside the standard Section 188/AOC-2 disclosure lens the audit checks — and so it appears nowhere in that audit's scoring, despite being, by a wide margin, the most consequential related-party-adjacent transaction this company has ever done.

The direction of that transaction appears to have favoured Sigma Solve Ltd's public shareholders: value moved from promoter-relative co-founders to the listed entity, for no cash outlay, and management was explicitly praised for it on the record by an analyst on the December 2023 call — praise this report has no basis to dispute. What is genuinely missing is the audit trail: no source found discloses the actual consideration, valuation basis, or funding mechanism behind Sigma Solve Inc.'s buyback of its own minority shareholders. A retail investor asked exactly this question on ValuePickr in November 2023 (Section 08) and received only a mechanical explanation of how a buyback works, never an answer to why the entity carrying nearly all the group's revenue was jointly held with promoter relatives in the first place, or on what terms that changed. This report cannot resolve that either — it is recorded here as an open item, not glossed over and not treated as a red flag it has no evidence to support.

What is otherwise clean

  • No litigation beyond the two SEBI matters above was found in this research.
  • No GST or income-tax notice was found.
  • No promoter share pledge was found.
  • Promoter holding stable at 73.2%, unchanged in every recent snapshot reviewed.
  • No KMP resignation was found beyond Prerak Parikh's formal director appointment in June 2025 (a governance-tidying step, not a departure).

14Verdict, and what would change our mind

A genuinely high-return business with real, unresolved credibility and disclosure gaps — priced for a partial recovery this report cannot confirm is happening

What we are confident about

Sigma Solve clears the coffee-can gates cleanly — a 9/9 "9iner" on revenue growth and ROCE — and cumulative cash generation over the decade (₹98.0 Cr CFO) exceeds cumulative profit (₹85.1 Cr), a 1.15× ratio that clears the standard cash-conversion gate once this pipeline's own capex-proxy distortion is corrected (Section 02). The related-party record is clean on the standard AOC-2 lens. Promoters hold a stable 73.2%, and the one large capital-structure event in the company's history — the consolidation of Sigma Solve Inc. into a wholly-owned subsidiary — appears, on the facts available, to have favoured public shareholders rather than extracted value from them. This is a genuinely capital-efficient, founder-controlled business that has grown from a ₹2 Cr revenue base to ₹96 Cr in under a decade.

What is a real, current weakness — not a data artefact

Three things earn that label, and they compound each other. First, EBITDA margin has fallen from a 40.5% peak (FY2023) to 29.5% (FY2026), three consecutive years of decline, and no management explanation exists on record for any of it — the company has not held an investor call since February 2024. Second, every specific, quantified commitment management made on its last two calls — FY2024 revenue guidance, margin sustainability, call frequency itself — has since been missed, with no later call to account for any of it (Section 09). Third, two SEBI adjudication orders landed in 2025: a real, if modest, IPO-proceeds compliance lapse (₹2 lakh penalty, company-specific), and a separate, still-apparently-open investigation into suspected fraudulent trading in the stock by outside entities SEBI links to a named group — not an accusation against the company, but a live fact about the integrity of trading in this name that a buyer needs to know (Section 11).
