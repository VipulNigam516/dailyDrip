Verdict

Quality: genuinely high, on every structural measure. Value: priced for management's optimistic aspiration, not its own current guidance.

Hexaware clears the coffee-can gates cleanly on a real, large capital base — a 9/9 "9iner" with ROCE holding 24-32% for a decade through a full change of control. Cash conversion, working capital and the related-party record are all clean. Relisted in February 2025 via a ₹8,750 Cr Offer for Sale (100% a Carlyle Group exit, zero proceeds to the company), the stock now trades ~30% below its ₹708 issue price.

The problem is guidance credibility. The first post-listing call framed "double-digit" CY2025 growth as "a solid baseline" — the year closed at 7.6%. Management's own words on the next call: "given how CY25 panned out, clearly, we have some lessons learnt on how to communicate with you." The reset CY2026 guidance was then cut again, from a stated "floor" of 7.6% to 6-7%, within weeks of this report. Twelve days before this report, the CEO who set every one of those commitments announced he is stepping down before CY2026 is proven out.

Run through a reverse DCF, ₹497 requires either a margin recovery beyond current guidance or growth near management's "low-teens" long-term aspiration — not its own 6-7% near-term number. The explicit bull scenario, a genuine but unproven recovery, lands almost exactly at today's price; simply holding current guidance flat implies roughly 14% downside. A clean, well-run business, a real and recent credibility gap, and a price that needs the aspiration, not the guidance, to come true.

01The business, and a genuinely unusual ownership history

A ~$1.6bn mid-tier IT major, taken private in 2021, sold between two global private-equity sponsors, and relisted via a fresh IPO in February 2025

Hexaware Technologies Limited is a Navi Mumbai-headquartered IT services company offering digital transformation, cloud, AI/automation and application services across banking & financial services, healthcare & insurance, high-tech & professional services, manufacturing & consumer, travel & transportation, and banking verticals, for a largely US- and Europe-based client base. On revenue of ₹13,430 Cr (CY2025), it sits below the "big five" Indian IT majors (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCLTech, Cognizant, each $10bn+) but is a genuine scale player in the tier analyst houses like Everest Group sometimes call "Top ITS Challengers" — materially larger than every other company this pipeline has covered to date.

Taken private, sold between two PE sponsors, relisted

Hexaware was previously listed, then taken private via a delisting completed around 2020-21 by Baring Private Equity Asia. Baring then sold its stake to an affiliate of The Carlyle Group (via an acquisition vehicle, CA Magnum Holdings) for approximately $3 billion in 2021. It is Carlyle's vehicle, not Baring, that has controlled Hexaware since. CA Magnum relisted the company via a ₹8,750 Cr Offer for Sale that opened 12 February 2025 and listed on 19 February 2025 at ₹708/share — entirely a secondary sale of existing shares; no primary capital was raised and the company itself received zero IPO proceeds. As of the most recent shareholding disclosure, CA Magnum (Carlyle) still holds roughly 74.3% of the company, classified as "Promoter" in exchange filings despite being a private-equity sponsor rather than a founder or family — a classification worth keeping in mind whenever this report or any data source uses the word "promoter" for Hexaware. Retail and HNI demand for the IPO was notably weak (0.11x and 0.20x subscribed respectively, against 9.09x from institutions) — the issue was rescued by QIB demand, not broad retail enthusiasm, and the stock now trades meaningfully below its issue price (Section 13).

This history has two direct, practical consequences for this report. First, Hexaware's own annual reports for FY2021-FY2023 do not exist in the form this pipeline (or any public filing search) can retrieve — the company had no public shareholders and hence no listed-company disclosure obligation for those years, a genuine gap in the record rather than a pipeline defect (Section 15). Second, the company now trades with barely seven months of continuous public history as of this report — its own management has openly said, on the record, that it is still learning how to set expectations with public shareholders (Section 09).

How the company reports: calendar year, not April-March

Unlike every other company this pipeline has covered, Hexaware's statutory fiscal year is the calendar year — confirmed directly from the FY2025 annual report itself, addressed "for the financial year ended December 31, 2025." Management refers to "CY24," "CY25" interchangeably with "FY24," "FY25" on every call, and this report follows that convention throughout: every "CY20XX" figure in this report is January-December of that year, not the April-March convention used elsewhere on this pipeline. (Third-party aggregators sometimes display a generic "Mar-20XX" column label for Hexaware out of habit; that label does not reflect the company's actual reporting period and should be disregarded wherever it appears.)

An extraordinary, real return on real capital — not a thin-base artefact

Unlike the small companies this pipeline has recently covered, Hexaware's high return ratios are not an artefact of a tiny capital base — this is a company with a genuine, substantial fixed-asset and working-capital footprint (net block alone grew from ₹479 Cr to ₹5,130 Cr over the decade, largely delivery campuses and, since FY2019, capitalised lease assets under Ind AS 116). ROCE has run in a stable 25-30% band for the past five years (Section 02), a genuinely durable level of capital efficiency for a company of this scale, not a number that collapses once the capital base is sense-checked.

What this report is really about

The central story here is not a governance or accounting question — the related-party record is clean (Section 11), and the business itself is real and well-run. It is a growth and communication question: CY2025, the company's first full calendar year back on the public market, delivered revenue growth of only 7.6% against a framing management itself set up as "double-digit... a solid baseline" at its very first post-listing call. Management's own words, on its most recent call: "given how CY25 panned out, clearly, we have some lessons learnt on how to communicate with you." That admission, and what it means for how much weight to put on the company's current guidance, is this report's central thread (Section 09).

A real margin step-down lands exactly inside the one window this report cannot see into

Operating margin fell from 15.8% (2021) to 13.3% (2022) — a single-year move this pipeline's own anomaly scan flags as more than double the company's typical year-to-year step. This is precisely the window in which Hexaware had no public shareholders and hence no annual report obligation: Carlyle completed its acquisition from Baring in mid-2021, and the company remained privately held through 2023. No primary source available to this report explains why operating margin fell in 2022 — consistent with this pipeline's binding rule that an unexplained anomaly gets a sourced cause or an explicit "not found," this is the "not found" case. Margin partially recovered from 2023 onward (15.1% → 15.3%) before the more recent, better-documented CY2025 compression covered in Section 04, which this report can explain because it happened entirely on the public record.

ROCE has held in a genuinely durable 24-32% band across the full decade — including through the margin dip — on a real, growing capital base (Section 02). That stability through a change of control, a full delisting cycle, and a margin compression episode is itself informative: whatever drove the 2022 margin move, it did not impair the underlying capital-efficiency of the business.

04Margins: a lower, AI-adjusted guide-down — disclosed candidly, but genuine

CY2026 guidance sits below CY2025's actual margin, on a metric management itself just changed. The reason given is not deleveraging or a one-off — it is a stated, quantified pricing effect from AI

CY2025 EBITDA margin came in at 17.1% (reported), up 120bps year-on-year and roughly in line with the 17.1-17.4% range management set at its first post-listing call. On the surface, CY2025 was a margin success. What changed materially is the outlook: starting Q1 CY2026, management switched its primary reported metric from EBITDA to EBIT ("based on what we see in the market," per the CEO) and guided CY2026 EBIT margin to 13-14% — a genuine reduction from the prior year's level on a comparable basis, not merely a metric-definition artefact, with management explicit that H1 will be weaker than H2 and that the full-year number is not meant to be read as a new permanent floor.

The Q4 CY2025 quarter, in detail — genuinely messy, and disclosed in full

Reported EBITDA for Q4 CY2025 was 17%, but management itself flagged that normalized for one-timers the quarter's real margin was 15.4%, a 210bps sequential drop. The one-timers were real and specific, not vague: a $25m earnout reversal and a $15m impairment charge tied to the underperforming Softcrylic acquisition (2024), a $4m additional expected-credit-loss provision, and a $3.5m accelerated lease-amortisation charge tied to a European restructuring — a combined ~160bps EBITDA-level headwind, plus a further ~64bps at the EBIT level, plus a ~$12.5m India labour-code cost expected to recur at ~20bps/year going forward. This level of itemised, dated disclosure is a genuine positive on transparency, even though the underlying quarter was weak.

The stated reason for the CY2026 guide-down: AI, quantified

Unlike every other company this pipeline has covered, Hexaware's own CEO has put a specific number on AI-driven pricing pressure, twice: on the Q4 CY2025 call, asked directly what an existing renewal looks like under AI, he answered "if the scope were to remain the same, it could be in that 30 to 40%... 20 to 40% depending on the type of work" reduction. In August 2026 press commentary (not a transcript this report read directly, but corroborated across five independent outlets), he is quoted putting the client-facing price effect at up to 25%, while estimating the underlying productivity gain at roughly 40% — leaving, in management's own framing, a "productivity gap" the vendor can still capture as margin if it executes well. Whether that gap is actually captured, rather than competed away as it has been at other Indian IT-services companies this pipeline has covered (Section 07), is an open, unresolved question — but the mechanism itself is stated by management in specific, quantified terms rather than left as vague industry commentary.

What would confirm the margin guidance holds, and what would contradict it

Confirming: EBIT margin exiting CY2026 above the 13-14% full-year range, as management's own "stronger H2 exit" framing implies. The Q1 CY2026 update (13% reported EBIT, flat normalized sequentially) is a modestly encouraging early data point — management explicitly said the quarter came in "better than we expected" on profitability. Contradicting: a full year that lands at or below the bottom of the guided range despite the stated H2 recovery, which would suggest the AI-pricing "productivity gap" is smaller in practice than management's own 40%-gross/25%-conceded framing implies.

05Working capital: a genuine strength, by management's own guided range and this pipeline's own numbers

One of the few areas of this report with no caveat attached

Receivable days (billed plus unbilled DSO, management's own preferred measure) have run 45-75 days across the full decade this pipeline can measure, with no sustained one-directional drift — a genuinely different pattern from every other company this pipeline has recently covered. Management has been explicit and specific about its own target range (70-72 days) and has beaten it in every recent quarter reported: 65 days (Q4 CY2024), 67 days (Q4 CY2025). OCF-to-EBITDA on an LTM basis has run 75-76%, again ahead of management's own stated 70% comfort range.

On growth and margin, a genuine mid-pack player — not a laggard, not a standout

Persistent Systems is the closest scale and business-model match (1.10×, digital-engineering positioning), and comfortably the stronger operator on every quality metric — 34.4% ROCE, 27.2% ROE, both meaningfully ahead of Hexaware's own 30.1%/24.9%, on faster revenue and profit growth. LTIMindtree, roughly three times Hexaware's size, shows what the next scale tier looks like: similar ROCE (29.6%) but a richer OPM (18% vs. 14%) — a reasonable "what does Hexaware look like once operating leverage from scale fully plays out" reference, if it gets there. Zensar and Birlasoft, both roughly 0.4× Hexaware's scale, sit at the lower end of the mid-tier band on every metric and are useful mainly as a floor, not an aspiration. eClerx posts this set's best margins and returns, but on a BPM/analytics model genuinely different from Hexaware's classic IT-services mix — a margin benchmark, not an operating comparable.

On ownership structure, the comparison that actually matters

Mphasis is the single most important name on this list, for a reason that has nothing to do with the numbers above: it is the closest available Indian precedent for a global private-equity sponsor holding controlling, "Promoter"-classified ownership of a listed IT-services company. Blackstone has controlled Mphasis since acquiring HP's stake in 2016, and — directly relevant to Section 11 — Blackstone's entire Mphasis stake is pledged, per Screener's own flags, the same structural feature this report found at CA Magnum Holdings' stake in Hexaware. Mphasis was never delisted, so it does not share Hexaware's specific gap in public trading history, but its return ratios (22.1% ROCE, 18.5% ROE, both softer than Hexaware's) and its own rising-debtor-days caution are a useful, sobering data point on what a mature PE-controlled IT-services company's numbers can look like once growth slows.

Sagility India — a US-healthcare-focused BPM company, EQT-sponsored, IPO'd via a pure Offer for Sale in November 2024 with the sponsor retaining 82.4% — is arguably an even closer ownership analog than Mphasis on one specific dimension: like Hexaware, it is a company investors are seeing on the public market for the first time under its current sponsor, not a decades-continuously-listed name a PE firm bought into later. Its business (US payer/provider healthcare outsourcing) is different enough from Hexaware's diversified BFSI/travel/manufacturing IT-services mix that it does not belong in the quantitative table above, but the pattern it represents — a global buyout fund relisting a majority stake it still controls, shortly after acquisition — is the same pattern this report is examining in Hexaware itself, and is worth knowing it is not unique to this one company.

07Growth outlook: a genuine pipeline, a guidance floor that has already moved once

A $4 billion pipeline and real deal wins sit alongside a second, more recent growth-guidance cut — worth tracking as a pattern, not a one-off

CY2025: the framing, and what actually happened

At its first post-listing call (March 2025), management described "double-digit" growth as "a solid baseline you can assume," while flagging that macro conditions had "trended marginally negative" in the two weeks since listing. By its next call (July 2025), the quarter had come in "a little bit softer than what we had anticipated, predominantly driven by delayed decision-making from customers." CY2025 closed at 7.6% reported revenue growth (7.1% constant currency) — below the "double-digit" framing set eleven months earlier, driven by a combination of macro/tariff-driven softness in Manufacturing & Consumer, two client bankruptcies, an underperforming 2024 acquisition (Softcrylic), and — management's own candid characterisation — a genuine forecasting miss on their part (Section 09).

CY2026: guided better than CY2025, then cut again within the year

At the Q4 CY2025 call (February 2026), management reset expectations more cautiously: CY2026 growth guided only to be "better than 7.6%," explicitly not treated as a new lower baseline, with the long-term "low-teens to mid-teens" thesis reaffirmed as intact. By the Q1 CY2026 call (May 2026), management reiterated "our guidance of 7.6%, which is a floor right now for us" after a quarter that came in "better than we expected" on profitability. By the Q2 CY2026 results (reported ~August 2026), that floor was cut to 6-7% — below the previous quarter's own stated floor — citing delayed deal ramp-ups (several large contracts pushed to start mid-to-late Q3) and a narrowing runway to execute delayed deals before year-end. The stock fell roughly 7-8% on the news. EBIT margin guidance (13-14%) was reaffirmed despite the revenue cut.

A pattern worth naming plainly

This is now the second time in three quarters that a growth number management called a "floor" has moved down rather than up. That does not mean the $4bn pipeline or the deal wins described below are not real — the evidence in this section says they are. It does mean that, per the guidance-credibility scorecard in Section 09, a reader should treat any specific forward growth number from this management team as a current best estimate rather than a number to anchor a valuation on without a margin of safety.

The deal pipeline is genuinely large, and increasingly diversified by type

Management reported the sales pipeline crossing $4 billion for the first time as of the Q4 CY2025 call, and Q4 2025 itself as "the best part of the quarter" on deal wins — including a large vendor-consolidation deal with a major technology company, a second-phase consolidation win at a large global bank, a full-stack engagement with what management described as the world's largest pet insurance holding company, a role in a large insurer's Guidewire core-platform modernisation, and an agentic-AI clinical-research-process deal. High-value ($10m+) client count grew from 32 to 34 in the most recent reported quarter. Two customers now exceed $100m in annual revenue (up from one a year earlier), and top-10 client concentration has moderated to ~36% even as marquee-account scale has grown (Section 02) — a genuinely healthy diversification trend sitting underneath the growth-guidance volatility.

Vertical detail: Banking and Healthcare & Insurance expected to lead CY2026; Professional Services lags

Per the Feb 2026 outlook, Banking and Healthcare & Insurance (H&I) are expected to grow "probably quite a bit higher than company average" in CY2026, Manufacturing & Consumer (M&C) is expected back to growth after a macro/tariff-driven CY2025 downturn, Financial Services and the newly-separated GTT (Global Travel & Transportation) vertical are expected at company average, and Professional Services is expected to grow but below company average, still absorbing the after-effects of two large account-specific issues (one large vendor-consolidation win still ramping, one large account that saw a "75% decline... year over year" before stabilising). The newly-created TPP (Technology, Products & Platforms) vertical, split out of the former HTPS reporting line starting Q1 CY2026, is expected to grow fastest but from a small base.

AI: a stated dampener on the existing book, and a stated source of new offerings

Management's own framing, repeated across multiple calls: AI is simultaneously compressing the value of existing, same-scope renewals (quantified at 20-40% depending on work type, Section 04) and creating new billable service lines Hexaware has moved quickly to launch — a legacy-modernisation platform (RapidX), a "vibe coding" offering (claimed first-to-market, July 2025), and a "Zero License" SaaS-replacement offering (launched January 2026, aimed at a stated $400bn SaaS market). When challenged directly by a JPMorgan analyst on whether Hexaware is more exposed to AI disruption than peers given a growth deceleration from "16% organic growth... to perhaps flat," the CEO's response was that CY2025's underperformance was not primarily an AI story but a combination of the factors above, while acknowledging an industry-wide AI growth "dampener" that the company has "accounted for" in its CY2026 numbers. Whether the new AI-native offerings grow fast enough to offset the dampener on the existing book is, by management's own account, still to be proven in the numbers rather than already visible in them.

08What institutional analysts have gotten wrong so far, and what they think now

Real, substantial sell-side coverage exists here — unlike this pipeline's usual small-cap subjects — and its own track record on this stock has been notably poor

Unanimous Buy ratings at listing, now stale against the price

Every specific brokerage initiation found in the months after the February 2025 relisting was a Buy, with price targets clustered between ₹820 and ₹950 — JM Financial (₹820), DAM Capital (₹820, 30× FY27E EPS), Kotak Institutional Equities (₹940), Motilal Oswal (₹950, 32× March-2027E EPS), Jefferies (₹930), and Nuvama (₹950, 30× CY27E P/E). Every one of these targets sits 50-90% above the price this report values the stock at (₹497). This is not a case of the stock simply not having moved toward a still-live target yet — it has fallen well below even the most conservative name on this list, and this report found no confirmation that any of the six formally revised its target downward, though that does not mean none has.

Current consensus: real coverage, genuinely split

As of early September 2026, 17 analysts cover the stock (cross-checked across two independent aggregators, Investing.com and MarketScreener, which returned identical underlying figures): 10 Buy, 3 Hold, 4 Sell, average 12-month target ₹594 (high ₹730, low ₹440) against a price of ₹497 — roughly 20% implied upside on the consensus average, but with a wide enough spread (and a real Sell minority) that "consensus" undersells the genuine disagreement among analysts who have actually modelled this stock. One valuation screen flagged Hexaware as trading at a premium to TCS and Infosys on a trailing P/E basis as recently as November 2025 — a fact worth weighing against the growth-guidance cuts documented in Section 07 before assuming the stock is cheap simply because it sits well below its IPO price and its initiation targets.

Retail forum coverage: thin, but the one voice that spoke up called it early

Unlike the institutional side, ValuePickr's coverage of the 2025 relisting is a single three-post thread, all posted the week before the IPO opened, with zero activity since listing day — a genuine gap for a company this size. The one substantive post, however, is worth reading in full: benchmarking Hexaware's revenue growth against Persistent and Coforge and applying a peer-multiple valuation framework, the poster calculated a fair value of ₹508-553 against the ₹708 issue price — "I am skeptical of their 'AI at its core' messaging since efficiency gains from AI should be reflected in profit margins but there has been no significant margin expansion" — and explicitly declined to subscribe. Eighteen months later, with the stock at ₹497, that single, off-the-cuff peer-multiple estimate has held up better than the sell-side's collective initiation-target cluster (Section 09 treats this the same way it treats management's own guidance: judged against what actually happened, not against how confidently it was stated).

10Capital allocation: the IPO itself was a PE exit, not a capital raise — the dividend since has been real and growing

Every rupee of the February 2025 offering went to Carlyle. Every rupee of dividend since has gone to shareholders, and the per-payment amount has grown

The relisting funded nobody but the seller

The ₹8,750 Cr February 2025 offering was 100% an Offer for Sale by CA Magnum Holdings (Carlyle) — no fresh shares were issued, and Hexaware itself received zero proceeds. This is a structurally different event from a typical growth IPO: it was a liquidity event for the private-equity sponsor, priced at ₹708/share against a cost basis reported around ₹385/share, implying Carlyle realised roughly 1.5-1.8× its investment on the shares sold while retaining ~74% of the company. None of this is unusual for a PE-sponsor exit, and it is not evidence of anything adverse to the company itself — but it does mean the IPO's purpose was never to fund Hexaware's growth, and any investor who bought expecting fresh capital to be deployed into the business misunderstood the transaction's structure from the outset.

11Governance: clean at the operating company, real leverage and a full share pledge one level up

Nothing found implicates Hexaware Technologies Ltd itself. The structural risk here sits at the controlling shareholder's own holding company, and it is worth understanding precisely

The related-party record is clean

The existing audit rates Hexaware 1/3 ("Watch") across every year available — the related-party universe is essentially the company's own wholly-owned foreign subsidiaries (routine intercompany services billing, working-capital loans), with no personal-interest counterparty found in any year, including the post-relisting FY2025 report read in full. AOC-2's non-arm's-length section reads "None" in every year available. The genuine limitation — FY2021-FY2023 have no public annual report to audit, because the company had no public shareholders during that window — is a real gap in the record, not a governance red flag; nothing found on either side of that gap suggests anything adverse happened inside it.

The 2020 delisting was, by the available evidence, executed fairly

Baring Private Equity Asia's 2020 take-private priced the exit at ₹475/share — 67% above the ₹264.97 regulatory floor and close to a ₹447.50 private reference price from a 2018 transaction cited by ValuePickr's own contemporaneous discussion (Section 12). No SEBI enforcement action, shareholder litigation, or NCLT challenge tied to the delisting was found in this research — an absence-of-evidence finding, not a confirmed clean bill, but one corroborated by genuinely bullish, opportunistic retail sentiment on the forum at the time rather than any hint of controversy.

The structural risk that does exist: CA Magnum Holdings' entire stake is pledged

A real overhang, one level above the listed company

In November 2025, CA Magnum Holdings (the Carlyle vehicle) pledged its entire 74.55% Hexaware stake (45.39 crore shares) as security for a $1.26 billion refinancing facility led by HSBC, structured to refinance debt originally taken on to fund the 2021 acquisition from Baring. This does not, on its own, signal an imminent further sale of shares — refinancing existing LBO-era debt is a routine treasury action for a PE-owned structure. But it does mean two things worth holding onto: a sharp, sustained decline in Hexaware's own share price could, in principle, trigger margin-call dynamics on that pledge, and the refinancing/rollover risk on that debt sits structurally above the listed entity, invisible in Hexaware's own (genuinely close to debt-free) standalone balance sheet.

This financing sits alongside a separate, and separately informative, data point: Fitch rated the CA Magnum Holdings / Hexaware group structure at 'BB-' (sub-investment grade), most recently affirmed with a Stable outlook, before withdrawing all ratings for commercial reasons around 1 December 2025. Fitch's own commentary put EBITDA net leverage at the group level at 3.7× in 2024, projected to ease to ~3.2× by end-2026, and explicitly flagged uncertainty over "whether capital management initiatives will lead to the upstreaming of cash to the private-equity sponsor, The Carlyle Group." That is a rating agency naming, in its own words, the exact tension this section is describing: a listed operating company that is itself nearly debt-free, sitting beneath a holding structure that is not, with an explicit question mark over whether cash moves upward to service that debt. No CRISIL, ICRA, CARE or India Ratings coverage of Hexaware Technologies Ltd itself was found — consistent with the operating company genuinely carrying little debt of its own.

An open Supreme Court tax matter, and one resolved favourably

A GST demand of ₹15.13 Cr (FY2020-21) was set aside entirely in Hexaware's favour by an appellate order communicated in May 2026 — a clean, resolved, favourable outcome. Separately, a more consequential income-tax reassessment dispute remains open: the Bombay High Court quashed certain post-2021 reassessment notices in Hexaware's favour, a ruling now cited as a precedent in Indian tax procedure; the Income Tax Department has appealed to the Supreme Court, with a hearing that was scheduled for 10 April 2026. This report could not find the outcome of that hearing — it should be treated as unresolved as of the report date (Section 15), and re-checked before relying on any assumption about its outcome.

What is otherwise clean

  • No SEBI enforcement action against Hexaware Technologies Ltd itself was found, distinct from the delisting-era procedural extension noted above.
