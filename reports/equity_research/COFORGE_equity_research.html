The one-paragraph version
A genuinely strong operating record, mid-integration of a transformative deal, priced closer to what this pipeline's own DCF can support than any other company scored this cycle — set against a live, unresolved governance shock

Coforge cleared every quantitative gate this pipeline tests — ROCE above 23% in every year measured, a perfect agreement between this pipeline's own ROCE formula and Vijay Malik's stricter cross-check (both qualify, 9/9 years), and a positive Gate 3 (cumulative cash flow from operations less capex less interest, +₹2,137 Cr over the decade) — the third company scored this cycle to clear all three cash gates simultaneously. It did this while closing two acquisitions five months apart: Cigniti Technologies, amalgamated 5 May 2026, and the much larger Encora Inc, a $2.35 billion, largely all-stock deal that closed 23 April 2026 and roughly doubled headcount overnight. The integration, on its own numbers one quarter in, is running ahead of the targets set at deal announcement. Two things complicate that story. First, this pipeline's own price cache was stale by about nine days and roughly 9% of share price at the point this research began: it still showed a pre-crisis price of ₹1,965.50 fetched 2 September, before Chairman O.P. Bhatt resigned on 8 September following an internal audit finding about his own board-evaluation scores, and the company's nomination-and-remuneration-committee chair resigned two days after that. This report uses the corrected, post-crisis price throughout. Second — and this is the more interesting valuation finding — even after that correction, this pipeline's own DCF bull case comes within about 2% of the current price, a materially narrower gap than this pipeline has found in any other company scored this cycle, because the market itself has already done some of the repricing this report's own arithmetic would otherwise have to argue for.

01The business: a mid-tier IT services firm that just did two deals in five months

Zero promoter holding, a combined entity roughly $2.5 billion in revenue, and a strategy built on immediate, complete integration rather than gradual absorption

Coforge Ltd (formerly NIIT Technologies, renamed 2021) is a mid-tier Indian IT services company providing digital engineering, cloud, data and AI-led services across BFSI, insurance, travel-transportation-hospitality (TTH), healthcare and public-sector clients, mostly outside India. Its ownership structure is genuinely unusual for this pipeline's peer set: as of June 2026 promoter holding was 0.00% — the company has no controlling shareholder at all. Institutional ownership fills the gap: FII 24.31%, DII 42.62%, public 33.07%. Every comparable this report uses later (LTIMindtree, Persistent Systems, Mphasis, Zensar, Birlasoft, L&T Technology Services, Hexaware) has some controlling or founder-linked holder in the 30–74% range. Coforge does not, a legacy of a private-equity-backed ownership history (Baring Private Equity Asia, after an earlier RPG Group association) that has left the register fully institutional.

Two acquisitions closed five months apart in FY2026 and define the company this report is analysing, more than any organic trend does:

  • Cigniti Technologies — a quality-assurance and software-testing specialist, amalgamated into Coforge on 5 May 2026.
  • Encora Inc — an enterprise value of $2.35 billion, closed 23 April 2026 and effective 1 May 2026. The deal was structured largely in stock: $1.89 billion of the consideration went to sellers Advent International and Warburg Pincus as preferential equity issued at ₹1,815.91 per share, alongside a $550 million loan raised to retire Encora's own existing term debt. Encora brought a strong Latin American nearshore delivery footprint and roughly 9,256 people into the combined headcount in the first two months alone.

The combined entity CRISIL now sizes at roughly $2.5 billion (~₹23,000 Cr) in revenue at a targeted 16–18% EBITDA margin — a step change from Coforge's own standalone FY2026 revenue of ₹16,477 Cr (+36% YoY, per CRISIL's July 2026 rating rationale). Management has been explicit and consistent about how it runs an acquired company from day one, in language repeated across at least three deals now:

"We immediately and fully integrate both organizations, ask leaders running the acquired firm to leave on Day 1 itself, so we have immediate and complete effective control."

— Management, Q1 FY2027 earnings call, 28 July 2026, describing the playbook also used on Cigniti, SLK Global and Incessant — verbatim, primary source

One quarter into the Encora integration, the numbers management has disclosed suggest the playbook is working faster than the deal's own original targets: 86% of combined revenue now comes from AI-led Engineering, Data & Integration and Cloud service lines, ahead of the 80% guided at deal announcement, and the combined entity's Q1 FY2027 EBIT margin of 16.0% already exceeds the full-year 15.5% guidance given for the combined business. Client concentration is moderate for a company this size — the top five clients are 18.0% of revenue, top ten 26.1%, with a granular tail below that (1 client above $100 Cr, 3 in the $50–100 Cr band, 14 in $20–50 Cr, 29 in $10–20 Cr).

Not organic growth

Every headline growth number this report quotes from FY2026 onward is materially inflated by the two acquisitions closing mid-year. Management's own disclosed organic figure — 5.2% constant-currency sequential growth, excluding both acquisitions and planned client-portfolio exits — is the cleaner number to track quarter to quarter, and this report uses it wherever the distinction matters, flagging the reported (acquisition-inclusive) figure separately.

02The checklist: full agreement between this pipeline's ROCE test and Vijay Malik's own stricter version

A Dirty 9iner, one year short of a clean sweep on revenue growth, and a rare case where the stricter cross-check does not narrow the qualifying years at all

This pipeline classifies Coforge a Dirty 9iner (tier rank 2): revenue growth cleared 10% in 8 of 9 measured years (the exception, FY2018, grew 6.76%), and ROCE cleared 15% in all 9 years, ranging 23.54–32.11% — never once dipping toward the threshold. Run through Vijay Malik's own stricter ROCE formula, which typically produces materially lower values than this pipeline's primary Screener-style calculation, the picture does not change: every one of those 9 years still qualifies, with values ranging 15.31–22.16%. That full agreement is unusual. Earlier companies scored this cycle (Dynacons Systems, Alufluoride) showed sharp divergence between the two formulas; the three most recent (APL Apollo Tubes, Dr Lal PathLabs, and now Coforge) all show the stricter formula agreeing closely or fully — a pattern consistent with these being larger, more capital-efficient, more heavily covered businesses than the earlier, thinner names this cycle has scored.

  • Organic growth 5.2% constant-currency sequential, excluding both the acquisition and planned client-portfolio exits — versus 1.1% reported sequential growth, the gap being the acquisition math and the deliberate exits.
  • Order book: $691M order intake in the quarter across 4 large deals; the next-twelve-month executable order book reached an all-time high of $2.23 billion, up 44.2% year-on-year from $1.55 billion.
  • Margin: consolidated EBIT margin 16.0% (organic-only 16.7%), already ahead of the 15.5% full-year combined-entity guidance given at deal announcement. Revenue grew 49% YoY, EBIT 101% YoY (414bps of margin expansion), PBT 92% YoY (284bps), PAT 110% YoY (271bps).
  • Headcount 46,228, up 10,451 net (9,256 from Encora, 1,195 organic); utilisation 82.5%; LTM attrition 10.4%, which management characterised as "among the lowest in the industry" — a claim this report has not independently benchmarked against the full peer set's own attrition disclosures.

A shrinking per-share earnings figure sitting inside a quarter of otherwise strong operating results is exactly the kind of number a growth-by-acquisition story can bury; this report treats it as a genuine, not cosmetic, dilution cost.
The integration, on one quarter's evidence

Encora's own standalone Q1 numbers inside the combined result: 20.3% EBITDA margin, 19.1% EBIT margin — both above Coforge's own historical standalone margin, which is unusual for an acquired business folded into a larger integrator and worth testing again once a full year of combined numbers exists. G&A synergy is tracking too: Coforge's own Q4 G&A of 6.7% plus Encora's 10% blending to a combined 6.6%, better than either standalone figure.

04Margins: a real operating gain, partly given back by a bigger balance sheet

The margin bridge separates what the business earned from what the two acquisitions changed underneath it

Pre-tax margin moved from 11.73% (FY2024) to 12.21% (FY2026), a modest 48-basis-point net gain over three years that hides much larger offsetting moves underneath. This pipeline's margin-bridge decomposition attributes the change to four components:

The EBITDA line is the real story, and it is genuinely operating. A +183.7bp improvement in operating margin over three years, on a revenue base that itself grew from ₹9,009 Cr to ₹16,403 Cr, is the single largest component and reflects the AI-led service-mix shift and deal-integration synergies management has described on calls, not a financial-engineering effect.

Depreciation is the acquisition's mechanical cost. Fixed assets on the balance sheet went from ₹2,376 Cr (FY2024) to ₹6,099 Cr (FY2025) to ₹6,763 Cr (FY2026) — almost tripling in two years as Cigniti and Encora's own asset bases, and the intangibles/goodwill recognised on acquisition, landed on Coforge's books. The resulting −85.8bp depreciation drag is the direct, largely unavoidable consequence of that balance-sheet expansion and should be expected to persist, not reverse, as the enlarged asset base continues to depreciate.

Other income is the line this report could not fully explain. It swung from a modest positive contributor to −₹82.6 Cr in FY2026 outright (see Section 03), a −86.0bp margin drag over the three-year window. The most plausible explanation is one-off acquisition-related costs or FX translation losses landing in this line around the Encora closing, but this report could not trace it to a specific disclosed item and states that as an open gap (Section 14) rather than asserting a cause it has not verified.

Interest cost improved slightly as a share of sales (+36.5bp margin contribution) even as the absolute interest bill rose from ₹134.7 Cr to ₹168.6 Cr — revenue simply grew faster. This is worth watching rather than banking: the $550 million Encora acquisition loan lands in FY2027 books, not yet reflected in any year in this table, and will test whether that favourable trend holds once a full year of the higher debt load is inside the numbers.

Asset turnover fell as the balance sheet grew

Net fixed-asset turnover dropped 26.6% over the full decade (3.31× to 2.43×), almost entirely concentrated in the two most recent years as the acquired asset base landed. Read alongside a rising, not falling, EBITDA margin, this is the opposite of Malik's subcontracting tell (falling turnover with falling margin) — it looks like acquisition-driven balance-sheet growth outpacing revenue integration in its first year, which is the expected pattern one quarter after a deal this size closes, not evidence of deteriorating unit economics.

05Working capital: receivable days drifting up, masked in the net cycle by even faster-growing payables

Two working-capital measures disagree here, and the disagreement is itself the finding

This pipeline's anomaly scan flags a sustained, decade-long drift in gross receivable days: 60.96 days (FY2017) to 88.34 days (FY2026), up 44.9% — not a single-year spike but a steady climb, the kind of trend that is easy to miss year to year and material in aggregate. Net working capital as a share of sales moved even more sharply, up 191.2% over the same window.

Read the net working-capital cycle (receivables plus inventory less payables, in days) instead, and the picture looks far calmer: it has actually improved from the high-40s (FY2021: 46.99 days) to the mid-30s (FY2026: 35.4 days) over the back half of the decade. The reconciliation is trade payables, which grew even faster than receivables in the most recent year — from ₹988.0 Cr (FY2025) to ₹1,775.0 Cr (FY2026), up 79.7%, plausibly reflecting both organic growth and the acquired businesses' own supplier terms landing on the consolidated balance sheet. Coforge is, in effect, extending customer collections more slowly while also taking more supplier credit, and the second trend has so far outpaced the first in the net figure.

Why this is worth watching, not dismissing

A net cycle that looks stable because payables are growing faster than receivables is not the same as receivables genuinely being collected faster. If payables growth normalises once the acquired entities' payment terms are integrated into Coforge's own supplier relationships — a one-time step rather than a repeatable trend — the underlying receivable-days drift would show through directly in the net cycle too. This report's own three-year incremental working-capital intensity is already elevated for an IT-services business: 19.7% (3-year) and 14.5% (5-year) — meaning close to a fifth of every incremental rupee of sales over the last three years has been absorbed into working capital rather than converted to cash, a genuinely high figure for a services company with no inventory to speak of (this pipeline's database shows zero inventory in every year).

This report could not attribute the receivable-days drift to a single disclosed cause — client mix (public-sector and government-adjacent clients, which typically carry slower payment cycles, are 6.0% of combined revenue per the Q1 FY2027 disclosure), acquisition consolidation effects, or genuine collection slippage are all plausible partial explanations, and this is recorded as an open item in Section 14 rather than resolved with an unverified cause.

07Growth outlook: a record order book, set against an industry starting to price GenAI deflation

Management's own guidance is already being beaten one quarter in; the harder question is what the whole sector is walking into

The nearest-term growth signal is unambiguous: the next-twelve-month executable order book stood at an all-time high $2.23 billion as of the Q1 FY2027 call, up 44.2% year-on-year from $1.55 billion, built from $691 million of fresh order intake across four large deals in the quarter alone. Management's FY2027 guidance — standalone Coforge EBIT margin of 16.5–17% (up from 14.4% the prior year) and 15.5% reported EBIT for the combined entity — was already exceeded in the very first quarter it applied to (16.0% consolidated EBIT margin actually delivered). That combination of a growing order book and guidance being beaten early is a genuinely strong near-term signal, on management's own numbers.

The medium-term picture is more contested, and this report treats an industry-wide headwind as exactly that rather than folding it into a company-specific view without evidence. Two structural themes surfaced in the background research, both dated August 2026 and both worth tracking rather than dismissing:

  • GenAI pricing deflation. Reuters reported (August 2026) that Persistent Systems' and TCS's own CEOs told the wire service clients are demanding 25–30% lower prices as AI tooling compresses the labour content of IT-services delivery, with roughly 80% of TCS's relevant contracts already moving to outcome-based pricing. Industry estimates cited put roughly 30% of the sector "at risk," with peak impact expected FY2026–28 — squarely inside this report's own DCF forecast window.
  • The proposed US H-1B fee. A $103,265 proposed fee (in comment period as of August 2026) would push the industry further toward offshore and nearshore delivery models rather than US-based visa-dependent staffing.
An inference, explicitly labelled as one

The background research agent's own reading — not a management statement, not independently verified by this report — is that Encora's strong Latin American nearshore presence could function as a structural hedge against both themes simultaneously: nearshore delivery sidesteps the H-1B cost pressure directly, and a services mix already leaning toward AI-augmented delivery (86% of combined revenue from AI-led service lines, per Section 01) is arguably better positioned for outcome-based repricing than a traditional staff-augmentation model. This is a plausible strategic reading of why the Encora deal was structured the way it was, not a confirmed management rationale, and this report flags it as inference rather than fact.

Set against the guidance and order-book strength, Coforge's own FY2027 combined margin guidance of 15.5% sits inside, not above, the CRISIL-estimated combined-entity long-run range of 16–18%, and management has not made a specific public statement this report found about how it expects GenAI-driven pricing pressure to net against the AI-led service-mix shift it is simultaneously pursuing. That tension — strong current momentum against an unquantified sector-wide repricing risk landing in the same multi-year window this report's DCF projects growth over — is carried into the valuation assumptions in Section 12 rather than resolved here.

08Management: a nine-year track record, and one quarter of evidence on the acquisition-era promises

CEO Sudhir Singh's team is in its tenth year; the combined-entity guidance ledger, by contrast, has exactly one data point

The current management team, led by CEO Sudhir Singh, is in its tenth year running Coforge and has a long-run record this report can score with real confidence: over the nine years this pipeline's own database covers, revenue compounded at 21.7%, EBIT at 24.6% and PAT at 24.1% — figures management cited on the Q1 FY2027 call that this pipeline's own independently computed revenue and PAT CAGRs corroborate closely (Section 03). That is a genuine, multi-cycle credibility base, and it matters directly for how much weight to put on the newer, acquisition-era guidance below.

The acquisition playbook itself has been applied consistently across four deals now — SLK Global, Incessant, Cigniti and, most recently, Encora — with management describing the same "immediate and complete effective control" approach each time (Section 01), rather than a fresh integration philosophy improvised for the largest deal in the company's history. That consistency is itself evidence of discipline, though this report has not independently traced the SLK Global or Incessant integrations' own financial outcomes to confirm the playbook's track record beyond management's own characterisation.

09Capital allocation: net cash going into the deal, a $550 million loan coming out of it

A decade of dividends funded entirely out of surplus cash flow, and a balance sheet about to carry acquisition debt for the first time at this scale

Over the full decade this pipeline's database covers, Coforge generated ₹8,014.9 Cr of cumulative operating cash flow against ₹6,364.5 Cr of cumulative PAT (1.26×), spent ₹5,249.6 Cr on capex and ₹628.8 Cr on interest, and still cleared Gate 3 — cash flow from operations less capex less interest — by a positive ₹2,136.5 Cr. That is the third company scored by this pipeline this cycle (after APL Apollo Tubes and Dr Lal PathLabs) to clear all three cash gates simultaneously, and it means the ₹2,276.9 Cr of cumulative dividends paid over the same period came out of genuine free cash flow rather than being financed by fresh borrowing or equity — the specific test Malik's third gate exists to apply.

The balance sheet going into the Encora deal was in a strong net-cash position: net debt moved from ₹390.8 Cr (FY2024) to ₹187.2 Cr (FY2025) to −₹371.4 Cr (FY2026, i.e. net cash), with CRISIL's July 2026 rating rationale separately confirming a cash surplus of ₹1,099 Cr as of 31 March 2026. None of this yet reflects the Encora transaction's own financing: the $550 million loan raised to retire Encora's pre-existing term debt lands in FY2027 books, and CRISIL's rating action itself enhanced Coforge's rated bank facilities from ₹989 Cr to ₹6,148.2 Cr specifically to accommodate it. This report's DCF (Section 12) builds in an estimated post-deal net debt figure rather than the FY2026 net-cash balance shown in this table, precisely because the FY2026 figures predate the debt.

A funding structure that limited new leverage

The Encora deal itself was financed mostly in stock ($1.89 billion of the $2.35 billion enterprise value, paid as preferential equity to Advent International and Warburg Pincus at ₹1,815.91/share) rather than debt — the $550 million loan retires Encora's own existing debt rather than funding the purchase price itself. That is a materially more conservative structure than an all-cash, fully-levered acquisition of the same size would have been, even though it comes at the cost of the roughly 25% equity dilution flagged in Section 08.

Credit ratings have moved in step with this discipline rather than against it: CRISIL rated Coforge AA/Positive (February 2025), upgraded to AA+/Stable (November 2025), reaffirmed that rating in January 2026, and reaffirmed again in July 2026 alongside the enhanced facility size for the Encora financing — a rating trajectory that improved, not deteriorated, through the run-up to the company's largest-ever acquisition (see Section 10 for the fuller credit-rating picture).

10Governance and credit: a chairman and the board's own NRC chair, both gone within 48 hours

A twelve-year clean related-party record, a rating trajectory that has only improved — and an unresolved boardroom transparency failure five weeks old at the time of this report

The September 2026 governance episode

Chairman O.P. Bhatt resigned on 8 September 2026. The trigger, per reporting on the episode, was a KPMG-led internal audit that found Bhatt's own board-evaluation scores — including the lowest rating given in the Chairman category — had been sent to Nomination and Remuneration Committee chair D.K. Singh but had not been shared with the full board. The stock fell 8.7–9% intraday the following day, 9 September. Two days after that, on 10 September, D.K. Singh — who had just accepted a fresh five-year term on the board — also resigned, stating: "the current circumstances may make it increasingly difficult for me to effectively fulfil my responsibilities." This report treats the episode plainly rather than either dismissing it as noise or reading more into it than the public record supports. What is not yet confirmed: whether the underlying governance process has been fixed, whether further departures follow, or whether the company's own "zero impact" characterisation holds up over the next one or two quarters. 13Verdict, and what would change our mind

Quality and value, kept on separate axes — this is not a buy or sell call

Quality: every gate cleared, an integration ahead of schedule, and a governance question still open

On this pipeline's own numbers, Coforge is a strong business: ROCE above 23% every year measured, full agreement between this pipeline's primary ROCE formula and Vijay Malik's stricter cross-check in all nine years (a rarity this cycle), a positive Gate 3 across the full decade (the third company scored this cycle to clear all three cash gates), and a genuinely disciplined, mostly stock-financed structure behind its largest-ever acquisition. One quarter into integrating Encora, the combined entity is beating its own margin and AI-mix targets, order book is at an all-time high, and credit ratings have improved through the whole process rather than pausing for it.

Set against that: a chairman and the board's own NRC chair both resigned within 48 hours of each other, five weeks before this report, over a stated failure to share board-evaluation results transparently — an unresolved governance question this report cannot yet close, on a company with no controlling promoter to anchor continuity through it. A live Mphasis lawsuit, a receivable-days trend quietly worsening beneath a stable-looking net working-capital cycle, and an unexplained swing in other income are each individually modest but collectively real open items (Section 14).

Value: the narrowest gap this pipeline has found this cycle, and still a gap

At roughly 41× trailing earnings, this report's own reverse DCF finds the current price requires either a 25.3% EBIT margin at management's own guided 16% growth, or 29.3% growth sustained for seven years at the guided 17% margin — both demanding, and both, unlike the last two companies this pipeline scored, not without precedent in the company's own recent numbers. The bull-case DCF (₹1,747) lands within 2% of the corrected current price (₹1,782) and close to the broker consensus target (₹1,716) — three independent reference points converging near the same figure. This does not make the stock cheap. It means the market has already done more of the repricing this report's own arithmetic would otherwise have to argue for, following the September governance shock and the demands the Encora integration places on the balance sheet. The base case, built on guidance the company is currently already exceeding, still sits 43% below the current price.

Specific triggers, with thresholds

  • Board composition and any further departures — the single nearest-term governance test; a stable board through the next one to two quarters, with the stated transparency process visibly fixed, would materially de-risk the open item in Section 10. A further senior departure would argue the opposite.
  • Combined-entity EBIT margin against the 15.5% FY2027 guidance — already beaten once (16.0%, Q1); a second and third consecutive quarter above guidance would start converting the acquisition-era guidance ledger (Section 08) from one data point into a real pattern.
  • Receivable days (Section 05) — whether the underlying gross drift shows through in the net working-capital cycle once the acquired entities' own payable terms stop growing faster than receivables.
  • The other-income line — whether the FY2026 swing to −₹82.6 Cr was a one-off acquisition cost that reverses, or a recurring drag; the FY2027 annual report should resolve this.
  • Mphasis litigation outcome — a settlement or dismissal would remove a live tail risk this report has not been able to quantify.

What would change this verdict: a quiet, stable one or two quarters — board composition settled, combined margins holding at or above guidance, receivable days stabilising — would let this report treat Coforge's operating and cash-conversion strength at close to face value against a price gap this pipeline already finds narrower than any other company scored this cycle. A further governance departure, or a margin miss against the guidance the company itself set five months into the deal, would argue the market's partial repricing so far has not gone far enough.

Chairman O.P. Bhatt's and NRC-chair D.K. Singh's resignations happened five weeks before this report was written.
