The one-paragraph version
A high-quality, acquisitive mid-tier engineer whose price already assumes its base case — and whose largest shareholder and board now matter as much as its margins

Coforge has compounded revenue at 21.7% a year for nine years, earns a pipeline ROCE of 19–29% every year, converted 1.26 times its profit into operating cash, held net cash until the April 2026 close of Encora, and is rated AA+/Stable by CRISIL. We read 17 earnings calls end to end and built a ledger of 49 quoted statements against later calls: management keeps what it controls (large-deal counts, margin targets, disclosure) and slips on forecasts (order-book conversion, ESOP cost, cash conversion), and it broke the pledge not to issue equity again within nineteen months. Growth is slowing organically — sequential constant-currency growth fell from 8.0% to 2.0% through FY26 — so the 44% rise in the headline order book to $2.23B should be read with caution: organic FY27 growth looks nearer 12–18% than 20%. Margins are genuinely better (EBIT 16.0% in Q1 FY27) but the Q4 FY26 exit rate leaned on currency and discretionary items, a wage hike has been deferred for fifteen months, and the definitions have changed three times in four quarters. Governance is the new variable: Advent and Warburg hold ~21% with board seats, voted a Chairman's reappointment below the 75% threshold in August, and within three weeks the Chairman and the NRC chair had resigned after an internal audit said evaluation information was withheld. On valuation, a plain DCF gives ₹1,393 in the base case against a price of ₹1,846.6; the price is justified only if that base case happens and the stock is still worth ~11 times EBITDA in seven years. This is a business worth owning at some price; whether this is that price depends on a terminal multiple, not on the next four quarters.

01The business: a mid-tier engineer that has bought its way past the middle

Services mix, clients, what "moat" means on the calls, and the one account that now matters most

Coforge Ltd (formerly NIIT Technologies, renamed in 2020) sells software engineering, data, cloud, automation and business-process services to banks, insurers, airlines and travel-technology platforms, mostly in the US (56.9% of FY26 revenue per CRISIL) and Europe (28.9%). On the Q1 FY2027 call it had 46,228 people (9,256 of them from Encora, which closed on 23 April 2026), quarterly revenue of $592.2M and an order book it called an all-time high. Its register has no promoter. The sponsor that ran it until 2023 (Baring Private Equity Asia/EQT) sold out in 2023, and in April 2026 the sellers of Encora — Advent International and Warburg Pincus — became the largest block holder at roughly 21% (section 12 covers what that block did at the August 2026 AGM).

“Consequently, Coforge has emerged as the only material Board governed professionally run Indian IT services firm in the industry.”

— Sudhir Singh (CEO), Q2 FY2024 earnings call, Oct 2023 — on the day the last private-equity stake was sold

What the company says it is

Management's self-description has been remarkably stable for nine years: execution, industry specialisation and engineering depth. The current team took over in 2017; the CEO's own framing of the starting point and the ambition:

“We were a $400 million firm when we started nine years back.”

— Sudhir Singh (CEO), Q4 FY2026 earnings call, 5 May 2026

The DB agrees with the arithmetic of the pitch. Screener-basis consolidated revenue grew from ₹2,802 Cr (FY2017) to ₹16,403 Cr (FY2026), a 21.7% nine-year CAGR (management cites 21.7%), with EBIT growing 22.2% a year and PAT 22.5% (management cites 24.6% and 24.1%; the PAT gap is a basis difference, not a contradiction we could close). Those are real numbers. What they hide is how much was bought: SLK Global (2021), Cigniti (2024), AdvantageGo (an in-house insurance-software product, sold 2025) and Encora (2026) all sit inside the series, and section 03 separates organic from acquired where the calls allow.

The moat, in management's words — and how to read it

“Reason number two is our outsized focus on driving growth essentially through solution based, proactive, large managed services deals.”

— Sudhir Singh (CEO), Q4 FY2025 earnings call, May 2025 — one of his "three reasons" for eight years of growth

“our teams count more on wallet share expansion and not client budget expansion as the primary arc for expanding our revenue.”

— Sudhir Singh (CEO), Q4 FY2025 earnings call, May 2025

The mechanism is wallet share inside a small number of very large accounts, won by proactive proposals rather than waiting for RFPs. It is a coherent strategy and the numbers fit it (top-5 clients grew 45.8% in FY26, accounts 6–10 grew 30%, per the Q4 FY26 call). The cost is that growth is lumpy and account-dependent, a point the 2024–26 calls keep returning to: a single banking client that "did not grow" took BFS to 12% growth in FY26, and an unnamed travel-technology client "underperformed severely" in FY24 before the same account came back as the $1.56 billion Sabre award.

“What slowed down our growth to only 12% for the current year, Kawaljeet, was the fact that one of our top three banking clients did not grow this year.”

— Sudhir Singh (CEO), Q4 FY2026 earnings call, 5 May 2026 — on why banking grew only 12% in FY26

“the largest account that we have in travel or had in travel in fiscal year in the travel tech space underperformed severely through the year because the business was under acute pressure.”

— Sudhir Singh (CEO), Q4 FY2024 earnings call, 2 May 2024 — on FY24; the account is not named on the call
What looks genuinely different

Attrition of 10.4% (Q1 FY27), which management calls "among the lowest" in the industry (we have not benchmarked it independently), repeat business is cited at 93% (Q2 FY24 call) and above 90% by CRISIL, utilisation has been between 79% and 82.5% on every call since mid-2023, and management has hired ahead of demand (indents are raised "120 to 130 days in advance of the hiring", Q2 FY25 call). The sales engine counts only non-renewal revenue for credit, and a "large deal" internally needs at least $20M of net-new TCV (Q1 FY26 call). Those are operating habits, not slogans.

Competition: a claim to flag

“in the real world, other than analyst reports, mid-tiers don't compete with mid-tiers. So firms of our size, if I look at 19 out of our top 20 clients, our biggest competitor there is a scale IT player. Could be anyone from Accenture to Deloitte to the TCSs of the world.”

— Sudhir Singh (CEO), Q2 FY2024 earnings call, Oct 2023 — a self-comparative claim; unverified by us

Treat this as a claim, not a fact: no named win/loss data is disclosed beyond the Sabre award, which management says it won against "two of the largest SIs in the world" (Q4 FY25 call). The peers the sell-side and ValuePickr actually compare Coforge with — Persistent, LTIMindtree, Mphasis, Hexaware, Zensar — are never named by management as competitors on any of the 17 calls, which is itself a finding for the peer section (Section 06).

The one-client question nobody has asked

The Sabre contract is $1.56 billion over 13 years. Dividing evenly gives roughly $120M a year, about 6% of FY26 revenue (the CEO cautions that "the revenues are never linear"), which would make it the single client above $100M on the Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 client-band tables. The analyst who raised Sabre's balance sheet got an answer about factoring and credit insurance but no revenue-share figure. Nobody asked what share of Q1 FY27 revenue it is; management has never volunteered it. That is the open concentration question behind the reassuring 18.0% top-5 figure.

Finding: dividends are only just covered when M&A is counted

On the conservative basis, the decade's cash after interest was ₹2,136 Cr against ₹2,277 Cr of dividends: a shortfall of ₹140 Cr. Put differently, every rupee spent on acquisitions and dividends over ten years was funded from operations plus the ₹2,240 Cr QIP of FY25 (CRISIL, Feb 2025). The earlier report's statement that dividends came 'out of genuine free cash flow' is true of the operating business and incomplete for the group.

NFAT against margin — Malik's subcontracting tell

Net fixed-asset turnover on the Screener basis fell from 3.3× (FY17) to 2.4× (FY26) while EBITDA margin was roughly flat to up. Malik reads falling turnover with falling margin as subcontracting; this is falling turnover with rising margin, so the tell is absent. The cause is accounting, not operations: goodwill and acquired intangibles sit in the Screener net block (net block went from ₹2,353 Cr to ₹6,096 Cr in FY25 when Cigniti consolidated; SLK alone added ₹315 Cr of intangibles and ₹615 Cr of goodwill, per the CFO on the Q1 FY22 call). The AI data-centre ($85M, five-year life) adds a small amount in FY25–26.

“the total consideration for 60% was INR 920 crores, total identified tangible assets, net of liabilities, were INR 192 crore. Identified intangible assets were valued at INR 315 crore. The goodwill that we recorded was INR 615 crores.”

— Ajay Kalra (CFO), Q1 FY2022 earnings call, 28 Jul 2021 — purchase price allocation for SLK Global, the first acquisition of the current era
Subcontracting is nonetheless live inside the P&L

The tell fails on the asset side but there is separate evidence on the cost side: the CFO said sub-contractor expense "shot up for us in Q1 [FY26] ... now started getting contained at $50 million to $51 million" a quarter (Q3 FY26 call), and Q4 FY26 listed a 50bp margin gain from "direct cost reduction due to the third-party cost". Cigniti was bought with 16% of its sales in contractor cost (Q4 FY24 Q&A). That is roughly $200M a year of third-party cost in a ~$1.9B business. It is a cost line, not a margin story, but it is the closest this company comes to Malik's tell.

Leverage

At March 2026 Screener-basis borrowings were ₹728 Cr (CRISIL: short-term borrowings ₹283 Cr, term loans ₹117 Cr, the rest leases) against cash of ₹1,099 Cr and equity of ₹9,538 Cr: net cash of ₹371 Cr. That is the pre-Encora balance sheet. The $550M term loan (fully drawn by 30 June 2026) turns it into roughly ₹4,400 Cr of net debt on our estimate. Total outside liabilities to tangible net worth is 0.56×, up from 0.43× in FY17 — the anomaly scan flags it, and the cause is in section 03.

04Margins: a real operating gain, a flattering exit rate, and three changes of definition

Bridge, management's margin walk, wage-hike timing and the accounting ladder

Coforge's operating margin has gone 17.1% → 16.0% → 16.1% → 14.0% → 17.9% (FY22–FY26, database basis). The answer to "did the business or the balance sheet improve?" is business, but less than the FY26 jump suggests, and the definitions used to describe it changed three times in four quarters (Q1 FY26, Q2 FY26, Q4 FY26).

— Sudhir Singh (CEO), Q4 FY2026 earnings call, 5 May 2026

Timing of wage hikes: the quiet margin lever

Wage hikes are the biggest annual hit to an Indian IT services margin (the CFO puts the Q4→Q1 dip at ~350bp, of which 220–250bp is wage hikes and ~100bp is visa and licence renewals). Coforge's own account of its practice has changed:

“We were possibly one of the few, if not the only tech services firm that on the 1st of April gave 100% of the increments.”

— Sudhir Singh (CEO), Q2 FY2024 earnings call, Oct 2023 — Q2 FY24: wage hike on 1 April, full, on time

“Wage increase in our case has happened effective the 1st of July. If it had happened effective 1st of April, we believe margins would have been depressed by 130 to 150 bps. Broad assumption would be 140 bps.”

— Sudhir Singh (CEO), Q1 FY2025 earnings call, 23 Jul 2024 — Q1 FY25: the same hike moved to 1 July

15Verdict: a high-quality franchise priced for its base case; quality and value kept apart

Two separate axes and the specific numbers that would move each

Quality: high on the operating franchise, with three live qualifiers

Coforge clears the checklist the pipeline applies: revenue growth above 10% in eight of nine years, pipeline ROCE of 19–29% every year, cash from operations above profit in nine of ten years (1.26× cumulatively), net cash before Encora, an AA+ rating, and a twelve-year clean related-party record (Section 02, Section 10, Section 12). The business model — wallet-share expansion in a few very large accounts, hiring ahead of demand, attrition among the lowest in the sector — is visible in the numbers, not only on the calls. Margins are improving in a way the peers' were not, though only some of it is operating (Section 04, Section 06).

The three qualifiers: (1) Management is reliable on what it controls and less so on forecasts — the ledger has fewer than half of its statements fully kept, two structural promises broken (no further equity dilution; no big bet abandoned) and a third, the US listing, shelved and written off (Section 09). (2) Governance is under live test — a 21% holder with board seats, a failed Chairman vote, two senior independents gone in 48 hours, an unexplained goodwill difference (Section 12). (3) Growth is part-bought and decelerating organically — sequential cc growth fell from 8.0% to 2.0% through FY26, with organic FY27 nearer mid-teens than twenty (Section 07).

Value: priced for the base case; the answer turns on the terminal multiple, not the next four quarters

At ₹1,846.6 the stock has risen 66% since the 31 March 2026 close and sits on 18× FY27E EV/EBITDA and ~32× our FY27E EPS. The plain DCF values the base case at ₹1,393 (25% below the price); exit-multiple versions put it at ₹1,966–2,489. The price needs roughly 22% annual growth at the guided margin, or the base case plus a mid-teens terminal multiple (Section 14). This is neither a deep-value price nor a bubble price: it is a price that leaves a thin margin of safety against a bear case (₹909 by DCF; 6.6% annual return at a 12× exit).

Verdict, in one line per axis

Quality: strong franchise, credible but imperfect management, governance under test. Value: priced for its base case; a margin of safety exists only if one believes in an exit multiple above ~11×. We do not give a buy or sell call.
